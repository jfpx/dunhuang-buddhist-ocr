# Barber (1990) Analysis: "The Unifying of rDzogs Pa Chen Po and Ch'an"

**Source:** A.W. Barber, "The Unifying of Rdzogs Pa Chen Po and Ch'an," *Chung-Hwa Buddhist Journal* Vol. 3 (April 1990), pp. 301–317.  
**URL:** http://ccbs.ntu.edu.tw/FULLTEXT/JR-BJ001/barber.htm  
**Full text retrieved:** Yes (complete paper, pp. 301–317 + notes)

---

## 1. Core Thesis

Barber argues that Ch'an Buddhism and rDzogs pa chen po (Dzogchen) were **formally unified** in Tibet through a specific historical lineage, culminating in the figure of **Rong Zom** (fl. c. 11th century, contemporary of Atiśa). The unification was not accidental but occurred through documented transmission lines that merged two generations before Rong Zom.

---

## 2. Three Transmission Lines of Ch'an into Tibet

Barber identifies three distinct Ch'an lineages entering Tibet:

| Line | Master | Brought by | Date | School |
|------|--------|------------|------|--------|
| 1st | Kim (Chin ho-shang) from I-chou | Sang Shi (son of Chinese commissioner) | c. 750 CE | Unspecified |
| 2nd | Wu Chu (無住) of Pao T'ang school | Tibetan minister Ye shes dbang po | After 750 CE | Pao T'ang |
| 3rd | Mo ho yen (摩訶衍 / Hwa shang Mahāyāna) from Tun Huang | Mo ho yen himself | Late 8th c. | Northern Ch'an + Pao T'ang hybrid |

### Key claims about each:

**Line 1 (Kim):** Sang Shi became **abbot of bSam yas Monastery** — "the central stage for the introduction of Buddhism into Tibet." A Ch'an master was thus the first Tibetan-born abbot of Tibet's most important monastery.

**Line 2 (Wu Chu / Pao T'ang):** "took on far greater importance." Wu Chu or students claimed transmission from Kim. The "radical teachings of the Pao T'ang school in some ways parallels the more radical approach taken in the Mahāsiddha's teachings which were being introduced from India."

**Line 3 (Mo ho yen):** "developed as much influence as that of the Pao T'ang line, if not more." Mo ho yen's teachings were "a mixture of both late Northern Ch'an and the Pao T'ang Ch'an." Barber notes four open questions about Mo ho yen:
1. Was he influenced by Pao T'ang or other schools besides Northern?
2. Did Tibetans inadvertently assign teachings to him that weren't his?
3. To what extent was he influenced by other Ch'an at Tun Huang?
4. Was he actually influenced by Mahāsiddha teachings?

## 3. Indian rDzogs chen Lineages

Two Indian masters transmitted Ati-yoga (rDzogs pa chen po) to Tibet:

| Master | Origin | Teacher | Area of influence |
|--------|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **Vairocana** | Tibetan monk | Śrī Siṃha (at Dhyānakāntaka, Krishna River) | **Kham** (eastern Tibet, near Chinese border) |
| **Vimalamitra** | Indian | Śrī Siṃha | **Central Tibet** |

> "Although it is reported that Vairocana only transmitted a portion of the tradition... upon scrutiny of the resources, it has been determined that Vairocana taught the entire tradition."

Traditional assignment: Vairocana = Mind Class (sems sde); Vimalamitra = Great Expanse + Instructions classes.

---

## 4. The Unification: Vairocana → Kham → A ro → Rong Zom

**This is Barber's central discovery.** The critical passage from Rong Zom's hagiography (Blue Annals):

> "Further, the (Lineage) which originated with Vairocana (was also received). This is one of the Lineages of the 'Mind Class (Sems sde)' (of the rDzogs chen teachings). At lDan gLong thang sGron ma there appeared an ascetic named **A ro Ye shes 'Byung gnas**, who possessed the secret precepts of the **seventh link in the chain of the Indian Lineage**, as well as those of the **seventh link of the Chinese lineage of Hwa shang (= ho shang)**. He preached the system to **Cog ro Zangs dKar mDzod khur** and to **Ta zi Bon ston**. These two taught it to Rong Zom. This (Lineage) is called the '(Lineage) of the Great Achievement (rDzogs chen) according to the **Kham method**.'"

### Lineage chain:
```
Indian line: Śrī Siṃha → Vairocana → [5 links] → A ro Ye shes 'Byung gnas
Chinese line: Hwa shang → [5 links] → A ro Ye shes 'Byung gnas  (MERGED)
                                            ↓
                              Cog ro Zangs dKar mDzod khur + Ta zi Bon ston
                                            ↓
                                        RONG ZOM
                                            ↓
                              (also received Vimalamitra lineage)
                              = COMPLETE rDzogs chen + Ch'an unified
```

> "Here we see that the lineage of the 'Mind class'... had been united with a form originating in China with its teacher identified as Hwa shang... These two lines of teaching were already associated with each other when Rong Zom received the teachings. Thus showing that **Ch'an and rDzogs pa chen po were united only two generations before Rong Zom**."

Barber notes: "Although it is tempting to associate this Hwa Shang with the famous Mo ho yen Hwa Shang, no evidence has come to light for this direct identification."

## 5. Doctrinal Parallels: Ch'an ↔ rDzogs chen

Barber identifies these specific correspondences:

| Feature | rDzogs pa chen po | Ch'an |
|---------|-------------------|-------|
| **Philosophy** | Yogācāra-Madhyamaka hybrid | Yogācāra-Madhyamaka hybrid (via Laṅkāvatāra + Diamond Sūtra) |
| **Sudden enlightenment** | Accepted | Central teaching |
| **Seeing mind's nature** | "Seeing the mind in its nakedness" (Mind Class) | "Original face" (roughly equivalent to "naked mind") |
| **No-thought** | No-thought → no-thought of no-thought | No-thought (wu-nian 無念) |
| **Non-conventionality** | "promoted a non-conventional approach to life as a Buddhist" | Non-conventionality |
| **Metaphysical base** | **Tantras** | **Sūtras** |

> "One can see... that there is considerable common ground between the Ati-yoga and Ch'an."

### Key difference:
> "In addition to the hybrid philosophical position held by the Ati-yoga tradition, its metaphysical base is firmly in the **Tantras**. Thus, explanations of meditational mechanics, modus operandi, metaphors... are all drawn from tantric literature. In contrast to this, Ch'an is firmly based in the **Sūtras**."

## 6. rDzogs chen Doctrinal Details (from Barber)

### Nine consciousnesses (rNyingma unique):
- 6 sense consciousnesses + manas
- **Ālayavijñāna** (storing capacity for karmic seeds)
- **Ālaya** ("all ground" — unaware aspect of Tathāgatagarbha)

### Tathāgatagarbha vs. Sugatagarbha:
- Tathāgatagarbha = possessed by unenlightened beings
- Sugatagarbha = same phenomenon, possessed by enlightened beings

### Meditation progression:
1. **No-thought** (initial by-product)
2. **No-thought of no-thought** (later development)
3. **Rig pa** — "pure pristine awareness... a state wherein one does not move from the non-dual"

### Three classes of rDzogs chen:
1. **Mind Class (sems sde)** — "teaches seeing the mind in its nakedness"
2. **Great Expanse Class (klong sde)** — "teaches the openness of being (= experiential aspect of śūnyatā)"
3. **Instructions Class (man ngag sde)** — "teaches the techniques for stabilizing and total incorporation of the overall view and experiences of enlightenment"

---

## 7. The Forgetting

> "Later Tibetan teachers forgot about this connection and went on to teach Ch'an in association with other teachings of rDzogs pa chen po. This association was so completely forgotten that in later years, when the rNyingma were accused of spreading teachings similar to Chinese thought (a major religious crime in Tibet), they would strongly deny such charges. In defence, they would point to the Indian origin of the rDzogs pa chen po."

## 8. Relation to Our Three Isomorphisms

### Isomorphism #1: Philosophical frame (Yogācāra-Madhyamaka hybrid)
Barber explicitly confirms both Ch'an and Dzogchen share a Yogācāra-Madhyamaka hybrid philosophy. He notes this parallel development occurred "at about the same time" in India and China and that "no research has been undertaken to investigate the connection."

### Isomorphism #2: Sky-gazing / seeing mind's nakedness
Barber's description of the Mind Class — "seeing the mind in its nakedness" — and the progression to "no-thought of no-thought" directly maps to the operational structure we identify in sky-gazing practices. The Mind Class is precisely where Ch'an was integrated ("Ch'an was associated with the mind class of the rDzogs pa chen po").

Key quote on visionary techniques: "There are visionary techniques used which make for a close contact with other tantra classes... even the visionary meditations are considerably simplified when compared with other classes of tantras. **The highest levels of practice are that of the direct viewing of the non-dual state.**"

This "direct viewing of the non-dual state" maps directly to trekchö practice and potentially to the operation described in T2834's 看淨 (kàn jìng).

### Isomorphism #3: No-thought → rig pa progression
Barber's description of the meditation progression — no-thought as "by-product," then no-thought of no-thought, then rig pa (pure pristine awareness) — describes the exact operational sequence we hypothesize connects:
- Ch'an 無念 (wu-nian) → T2834's 看淨
- Dzogchen trekchö → rigpa recognition
- The "by-product" framing of no-thought is critical: it's not the goal but the gateway

## 9. Texts Mentioned

| Text | Context |
|------|---------|
| **bSam gtan mig sgron** | Not explicitly mentioned by Barber (significant absence) |
| **Laṅkāvatāra Sūtra** | Basis for Ch'an's Yogācāra-Madhyamaka hybrid |
| **Vajracchedikā (Diamond Sūtra)** | Basis for Ch'an's Madhyamaka component |
| **Karaṇḍavyūha Sūtra** | Legend of falling from sky in early Tibet |
| **Guhyagarbha Tantra** | Translated by Ācārya associated with Rong Zom's previous life |
| **rDzogs pa Chen po'i lTa sgom Man ngag** | Rong Zom's own work on Dzogchen theory and practice |
| **Blue Annals** | Source for Rong Zom hagiography and lineage documentation |
| Tun Huang manuscripts | General reference to Ch'an-Tibetan translation corpus |

**Note:** T2834 (觀心論 / 看心看淨) is NOT mentioned by Barber. This is relevant because the practice-level comparison Barber makes is at the doctrinal level, not at the level of specific technique manuals.
