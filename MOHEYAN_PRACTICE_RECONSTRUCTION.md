## 2. Moheyan's Practice: What We Know from Barber

Barber does **not** give Moheyan's specific practice instructions but provides critical contextual framing:

- Mo ho yen taught a **hybrid** of late Northern Ch'an and Pao T'ang Ch'an
- He was based at **Tun Huang** (Dunhuang)
- Northern Ch'an emphasized: **看心 (kàn xīn)** — looking at mind; gradual purification
- Pao T'ang Ch'an emphasized: **無念 (wú niàn)** — no-thought; radical non-engagement
- The hybrid would combine observation technique (Northern) with no-thought goal (Pao T'ang)

Barber's key question: "if the Tibetans had inadvertently assigned teachings to Mo ho yen that were not representative of his position" — suggesting the Tibetan accounts may conflate multiple Ch'an streams.

## 3. Moheyan's Practice: Reconstruction from Dunhuang Sources

### 3.1 From Tibetan Dunhuang manuscripts (IOL Tib J 468 and related)

Based on published scholarship (van Schaik, Gómez, Broughton), Moheyan's instructions as preserved in Tibetan include:

**Core instruction (paraphrased from secondary sources):**
> Do not examine anything. Do not think about anything. Do not analyze. Do not meditate. Do not reflect. Rest in the nature just as it is. Do not direct the mind.

**Three types of practitioners identified by Moheyan:**
1. **Superior** — Can enter non-conceptual absorption directly without any support
2. **Middling** — Use watching/observing the mind as entry point, then let go
3. **Inferior** — Use a question or investigation as initial support, then release

### 3.2 The Northern Ch'an component (T2834 / 觀心論 context)

T2834 and related Northern Ch'an texts teach 看淨 (kàn jìng — looking at purity) or 看心 (kàn xīn — looking at mind):

**Operation:** Direct the attention toward the mind itself. Observe without grasping. The "looking" is non-conceptual observation. As conceptual thought subsides, the natural purity (清淨 qīngjìng) of mind is revealed.

**Key features:**
- Active verb: 看 (kàn) = to look, to watch, to gaze
- Object: 心 (xīn) = mind; or 淨 (jìng) = purity/clarity
- Modality: visual/attentional, not discursive
- Progressive: initial looking → settling → recognition of clarity

### 3.3 The Pao T'ang component (Wu Chu's teaching)

From Barber's earlier work "No-Thought in Pao T'ang Ch'an & Early Ati-yoga" (JIABS 1986):
- **Wu-nian (無念)** as practice: complete cessation of intentional mental activity
- No practices, no precepts, no rituals — radical simplicity
- "Dharani Gate" reference suggests possible tantric contact

## 4. Operation-Level Comparison: Chan × Dzogchen × Moheyan

### 4.1 Three-way comparison table

| Dimension | Northern Ch'an (T2834) | Moheyan (hybrid) | Dzogchen trekchö |
|-----------|----------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Initial instruction** | 看心 — look at mind | Don't examine, don't think, rest as-is | Look into the nature of whatever arises |
| **Attention modality** | Visual-attentional gaze | Release of all directed attention | Open awareness, often with sky-gazing |
| **Conceptual activity** | Let thoughts settle through observation | Do not engage thoughts at all | Self-liberate thoughts as they arise |
| **Goal state** | 清淨 (natural purity revealed) | Non-conceptual absorption (mi rtog pa) | Rig pa (pristine awareness) |
| **Body posture** | Sitting (implied) | Not specified in extant sources | Specific postures including upward gaze |
| **Metaphysical frame** | Sūtra-based | Sūtra-based | Tantra-based |
| **Practitioner types** | Single method for all | Three types (superior/middling/inferior) | Introduced pointing-out varies by capacity |

### 4.2 The critical isomorphism: "looking" as operation

The shared operation across all three:

```
T2834:     看 (kàn)    → 心/淨 (mind/purity)  → 清淨 revealed
Moheyan:   observe     → mind-as-is            → non-conceptual state  
Trekchö:   look/gaze   → nature of mind        → rig pa recognized
```

**All three use a visual-attentional metaphor for the core operation.** The verb is not "think about" or "analyze" but "look at" / "gaze upon." This is not coincidental — it reflects a shared understanding that:

1. The mind's nature is **already present** (not constructed)
2. It is **seen** (not reasoned to)
3. The seeing requires **relaxation** of conceptual overlay
4. The result is described in luminosity terms (淨/purity, clarity, rig pa)

### 4.3 Where they diverge

| Ch'an (T2834) | Moheyan | Dzogchen |
|----------------|---------|----------|
| Looking is sustained effort | Looking is abandoned for pure resting | Looking and resting are simultaneous |
| No tantric framework | No tantric framework | Full tantric framework (channels, winds, drops) |
| No visionary component | No visionary component | **Tögal** adds visionary lights, thig le, etc. |
| No pointing-out | No pointing-out | Requires pointing-out (ngo sprod) from master |
| Progress through gradual clearing | Instant — or not at all | Recognition is instant; stabilization is gradual |

## 5. Sky-Gazing as Bridge Practice

### The Dzogchen sky-gazing instruction:
Gaze into the open sky (preferably blue, cloudless). Allow the visual field to become the support for releasing conceptual mind. As fixation dissolves, awareness recognizes itself.

### Connection to T2834's 看淨:
- 淨 (jìng) = purity/clarity — the sky is the natural metaphor
- 看 (kàn) = to gaze — the same attentional operation
- The sky provides a **non-object**: nothing to fixate on, so fixation dissolves
- This is functionally identical to the Dzogchen instruction

### Connection to Moheyan:
- "Do not examine anything" — sky provides nothing to examine
- "Rest in the nature as it is" — sky creates the conditions for this
- Moheyan's superior practitioner needs "no support" — but middling practitioners use observation as entry, and sky provides the minimal support

### Hypothesis:
Sky-gazing may be the **missing practice link** that Barber's doctrinal analysis implies but doesn't specify. The Mind Class (sems sde) — where Ch'an was integrated — teaches "seeing the mind in its nakedness." Sky-gazing is the operational technique for achieving this in the Dzogchen context.

## 6. The bSam gtan mig sgron Connection

Nubchen Sangye Yeshe's *bSam gtan mig sgron* (Lamp for the Eye of Meditation, c. 9th century) is the key missing text from Barber's analysis. It:

1. **Explicitly compares** Ch'an, gradual, Mahāyoga, and Atiyoga meditation
2. Was composed **during the period** when Chan and Dzogchen coexisted in Tibet
3. Treats Ch'an meditation **sympathetically** while placing Dzogchen above it
4. Preserves Ch'an practice instructions in Tibetan translation

This text would be the primary source for reconstructing the **practice-level** relationship that Barber documents at the lineage level.

## 7. Open Questions

1. **T2834 specifically:** Does any Dunhuang manuscript contain a Tibetan translation of the 觀心論 or the 看心看淨 instructions? If so, this would be direct evidence of the practice bridge.

2. **A ro Ye shes 'Byung gnas:** What specific practices did this figure transmit in the "Kham method"? He held both Indian and Chinese lineages — his practice instructions would show the merger in action.

3. **Sky-gazing in Chan:** Is there any evidence of sky-gazing or analogous open-field visual practice in Northern Ch'an or Pao T'ang materials from Dunhuang?

4. **Rong Zom's practice manual:** His *rDzogs pa Chen po'i lTa sgom Man ngag* (Precepts on Theory and Meditative Practice) should contain the integrated practice. Has it been translated?
